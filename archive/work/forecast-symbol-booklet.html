The brief

Design a custom set of ten vector symbols around a single theme, then present them in a 24-page, 5 × 5 inch booklet, paired with photography and minimal supporting text.

I chose weather. Weather shapes the natural world and our daily lives, influencing everything from the landscapes around us to how we dress. Some symbols represent major climate types, such as the desert and tundra; others highlight natural elements like wind, water and fire. Temperature, raincoat and mittens show how people adapt to weather, growth shows nature’s response to it, and the closed umbrella symbolizes readiness for unpredictable weather.

Filled, for print

After class critique I landed on filled symbols. Filled shapes are the easiest to print consistently, and solid fills gave the strongest contrast, pulling the ten into one system.

The palette

The bright primaries gave way to slate, ochre, steel blue, olive and teal, colours pulled toward the tones of the photographs, and the set was named Forecast.

Outcome

Everyday things, made into ideas.

The project included research, brainstorming, sketching and refining the symbols with attention to abstraction, unity and graphic clarity. Limited colour palettes, two per spread, kept the booklet consistent.

The goal was to explore how symbolism and design can elevate everyday objects into visually engaging, idea-driven compositions. Together the ten symbols illustrate the connection between climate, nature and human adaptation.
