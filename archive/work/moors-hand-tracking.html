The brief

Hand tracking was a piece I proposed and added to the lobby. A webcam follows the visitor’s hand, so they can pick up props from the show on a 3D stage.

The sign beside it says it all: “No buttons, no touching. Just raise your hand in front of the screen. A camera follows your hand and turns it into a glowing hand on stage.” And underneath: “The camera only follows your hand. Nothing is recorded or saved.”

Versions

Three versions, less to learn each time.

Version 1 waited for a touch. Version 2 needed no touching at all: raise a hand and the props are yours. The final version added a game: how to stand, a warm-up, then four cues to set the scene.

The game

Four cues before curtain.

The four cues

  • Signal the stage crew. Wave the handkerchief.
  • Set the tea. Carry the teacup to the glowing mark. Rushing it spills.
  • Light the campfire. Hold the s’more until it catches fire, then shake it out.
  • Tie it all together. Twirl the ribbon in a circle.

Then free play

  • “Places, everyone.” Every prop unlocks and the stage is theirs.
  • Each prop changes the scene behind it: the teacup a parlour, the s’more a campfire, the handkerchief an old map, the ribbon a misty lake.

Built to run

Left alone for the whole show run.

What keeps it running

  • It reloads itself every six hours, waiting for a quiet moment with nobody in front of it.
  • A lighter standby while nobody is there, full speed the moment a hand appears.
  • It runs without internet, on the computer in the space.

What people said

  • “Seeing the hand movements on the computer really makes me think about how I actually move my hand. It is really interesting to look at it from that perspective.” A first-year design student, at the design opening.
