The brief

A series of posters for NASA’s Mars missions. It started with research: on March 13 I presented what I found about Opportunity, Curiosity and InSight.

What each one set out to do, where it landed and what it found became the content of each poster.

Seven steps, research to print.

From a research presentation to a printed series in under three weeks.

Following the tracks

Round 3. Feedback pushed the idea further: instead of drawing the rover, show only what it leaves behind, its tracks.

7April 1

The final series

The final set was picked through what resonated most in critique with my peers and Professor Gericke.

Opportunity explored the Martian terrain for almost 15 years, far beyond its planned 90 days.

The largest and most capable rover ever sent to Mars.

A lander built to study the “inner space” of Mars: its crust, mantle and core.

The final series printed and pinned up, April 1.

The idea

“Show what the rover leaves behind.”

That feedback became the series. The curved bands are tracks, and the black is the rover alone in the vacuum of space. Each rover gets one primary color of light, the only primary colors on Mars without people.

Red, for Opportunity.

Green, for Curiosity.

Blue, for InSight.

Typography

Connection against distance.

After the review I chose type that shows connection, against the distance of the black background. Letters in each name share a stroke.

The u and the n share a stroke.

The u and the r share a stroke.

The g and the h share a stroke.
