The brief

Laborde Products is a marine engine distributor in Covington, Louisiana, with locations in Paducah, Kentucky, and Houston, Texas. As their graphic design intern, every day was different: print ads, product guides, internal documents, apparel, social media and a mural.

I came in wanting three things: to put my studio training to work on real deliverables that get printed, published and used; to design for an audience outside the design world; and to grow as a professional, not just as a designer.

The print ads and the Mitsubishi Marine Power Guide have their own pages. This page is the rest of the summer.

Close-up

An 8 × 22 ft wall, in under two days.

A mural for a conference room at Laborde’s Deer Park, Texas office: a custom image collage with the company’s mission statement and PACT values across the full length. From the brief to four concepts with in-context mockups, feedback, production-ready artwork and coordinating with the installer in Texas, the whole project took under two days. The artwork itself went from concept to install-ready in under 24 hours.

Rapid iteration is where I felt my skills grow the most: balancing creativity, collaboration and speed while keeping the quality high.

Speed was the point. As a designer working alongside AI, I have to work quickly, or the work will simply be done by AI instead.

Close-up

One handbook, two layouts.

My very first project was the team member handbook, a 40-page book in InDesign, my weakest Adobe program at the time. Instead of struggling through it alone, I asked my supervisor for a better method, and adapting her workflow is what let me handle a file that large. Knowing when to ask was as valuable as the technical skill.

The original booklet worked in print but was hard to navigate on screen, so I set the same content in two layouts: a print booklet and a single-column digital version.

754 slides, one system.

The biggest project of the summer was a complete overhaul of Laborde’s Mitsubishi engine training course. The existing files were too inconsistent to batch-process, so I rebuilt 754 slides on a structured template system that put accuracy, consistency and the brand first. I also made presentation themes for the manufacturers Laborde represents.

The challenge

Proving the value of a designer.

AI can generate designs almost instantly, so my real challenge was showing my supervisor and the owner that there is still a genuine need for human designers.

I leaned into rapid iteration, many concepts, fast, to match AI on speed. I focused on what AI does poorly: brand consistency, understanding this client, and the judgment to know what fits Laborde. And I presented developed concepts so people saw a designer’s thinking, not just a file. I also brought AI into my own workflow to speed up ideas and iterations, while keeping the creative direction and final judgment my own.

Reflection

Design for the people who come after you.

Some of the most valuable design work isn’t the most noticeable. It’s the systems and templates that help other people work faster: the congratulations ad templates, the training system, and a SharePoint archive I reorganized so the team won’t hit broken links later.

I came in split between in-house and agency work, and found I love in-house design: one familiar client to focus everything on. I’m returning to Loyola with a stronger portfolio and a better sense of how design works outside the university.
