The brief

Design an opening title for a live-action film released before 1990. It should feel authentic to its time, with type, pacing and techniques that could have been made when the film came out, before digital typefaces and After Effects.

My title for Earth vs. the Flying Saucers plays like a military briefing: blueprints and diagrams of what the government in the movie knows about the aliens and how they fly.

I drew the blueprints in Illustrator, printed them to scale on transparency paper and mounted them as 35 mm slides. Then I projected them with a 1950s slide projector, filmed the projection and edited the footage in After Effects.

1Review 1

Three films

Three concepts, each sketched in my sketchbook and then laid out as a six-frame storyboard. I went with Earth vs. the Flying Saucers, the film I had already made a trailer for, so the two projects would be cohesive.

Key scenes from the movie turned into vector graphics, with the camera moving between them.

Colored circles on a grid shift to squares and frame the text, after Maurice Binder’s 1962 title.

Numbers built from dots count up to 11, then turn into 1960s casino graphics.

2Review 2

A typeface from before 1956

The type had to exist when the film came out. I set the full cast and crew in Microgramma, Futura and Bank Gothic, in regular and bold, title case and uppercase.

4Research

Documents from before 1956

With permission, I researched released U.S. government documents about UFOs that date to before the movie. Some technical drawings appear in the film for only a split second, so I took screenshots and redrew them.

This memorandum became the background of the main title.

The edit

The raw iPhone footage shows everything in front of the lens: the edges of the projection, the dark room, shadows crossing the light. Cut 01 put every credit in order. For Review 8 I stabilized each clip and added music composed before the film was made.

Reflection

The time machine.

The goal was an opening that feels like a military briefing from 1956, with all of the information the government in the movie has, including diagrams of how the saucers fly.

A big part of the project was imagining I was back in time and using as many old tools as possible: a typeface from 1930, slides, a real projector, and music composed before the film was made.
