The brief

Each student was given a poster from the Container Corporation of America’s Great Ideas series and made an animated collaboration with its designer, for a show where every piece played on a loop.

Bayer’s poster sets a line by Ludwig Wittgenstein: “the limits of my language mean the limits of my world.” Language as a limit brought me to the oldest story about it, the Tower of Babel. The blocks build the tower, the tower comes down, and the blocks become the poster.

The poster had to play a central role, every movement had to serve the story, and because the pieces played on a loop together, the animation had to clear the screen at the end.

1September 18

The poster I was given

Posters from the Container Corporation of America’s Great Ideas series were assigned by spinning a wheel in class. Mine came up Herbert Bayer. Bayer stacks frames inside frames, each topped with a strip of sky, and sets Wittgenstein’s line in four short lines underneath.

For the Container Corporation of America.

2Rebuild

Taking it apart

Before animating, I remade the poster from the scan: the solid colors as new shapes, each strip of clouds cropped into its own file so it could move later, and the type set in Helvetica, the closest match I found.

Layered for animation.

3Storyboards

Three ideas, then one

Three storyboards from the poster’s blocks: frames that open and close, a tower stacked from the blocks, and blocks scattered across the page. The final combines the scattered blocks and the tower into one story.

The blocks gather, stack into a tower, come down into the poster’s frames, and the type appears.

7October 30

Moving clouds

Professor Normand liked how the boxes came together so isometrically from the tower, and pushed for moving clouds inside the boxes. I left it until the end, but got it done: each strip of clouds is its own precomp, so the sky drifts inside every frame.

Moving clouds inside every frame.

Every block is its own layer, keyframed by position.

Close-up

The type behind a shield.

A big part of the project was working out how to make the poster’s type appear. The blocks act like a shield: as the biggest block passes over the bottom of the poster, the text appears behind it, so it feels natural for the type to show up.

The tower comes down over the bottom of the poster.

Reflection

A tower of blocks that falls, and becomes the poster.

The quote on the poster is about language as a limit, which brought me to the Tower of Babel. Working out how the type appears was a big part of the project, and the moving clouds Professor Normand pushed for came last, but they made it.
