The brief

Each student was given a poster from the Container Corporation of America’s Great Ideas series and made an animated collaboration with its designer, for a show where every piece played on a loop.

Bayer’s poster sets a line by Ludwig Wittgenstein: “the limits of my language mean the limits of my world.” Language as a limit brought me to the oldest story about it, the Tower of Babel. The blocks build the tower, the tower comes down, and the blocks become the poster.

The poster had to play a central role, every movement had to serve the story, and because the pieces played on a loop together, the animation had to clear the screen at the end.

Posters from the Container Corporation of America’s Great Ideas series were assigned by spinning a wheel in class.

Before animating, I remade the poster from the scan: the solid colors as new shapes, each strip of clouds cropped into its own file so it could move later, and the type set in Helv

Three storyboards from the poster’s blocks: frames that open and close, a tower stacked from the blocks, and blocks scattered across the page.

The first full build in After Effects, from the scattered blocks to the poster.

Sound, and an ending that clears the screen.

I kept working on the ending: the inner blocks pull out of the frames before everything lifts off.

Professor Normand liked how the boxes came together so isometrically from the tower, and pushed for moving clouds inside the boxes.

Reflection

A tower of blocks that falls, and becomes the poster.

The quote on the poster is about language as a limit, which brought me to the Tower of Babel. Working out how the type appears was a big part of the project, and the moving clouds Professor Normand pushed for came last, but they made it.
